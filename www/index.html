    How to pay tax you owe

    With PAYE jobs you don’t send HMRC a bill yourself. HMRC works it out and tells you how to pay. Here’s what happens and what you can do.

    Fix it now, during the tax year

    1. Open the HMRC app or your Personal Tax Account and check the tax code for each job.
    2. Update the estimated yearly pay for each job if it’s wrong. HMRC will adjust your codes.
    3. If your main job earns less than your allowance, ask HMRC to split the allowance between jobs.
    4. The new codes spread any shortfall over the rest of the year, so there’s no lump sum later.

    After the tax year ends (5 April)

    1. HMRC checks your records. If you paid the wrong amount, it sends a P800 tax calculation, usually between June and the end of November.
    2. Owe under £3,000 and still in PAYE work: it’s normally collected through your tax code over the next tax year, a bit each payday. You can choose to pay it in one go online instead.
    3. Owe £3,000 or more, or it can’t come through your tax code: you get a Simple Assessment letter. Pay by the deadline on the letter, usually 31 January.
    4. Pay using the reference on your letter, through the HMRC app, online or telephone banking, or by debit card on GOV.UK.

    If you’re owed a refund

    When your P800 arrives, claim it in the HMRC app or your Personal Tax Account and it’s paid into your bank. If you don’t claim it, HMRC sends a cheque.

    If you’re self-employed

    1. Register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of your first tax year of self-employment.
    2. Send your return online by 31 January after the tax year ends, and pay what you owe by the same date.
    3. If your bill is over £1,000, and less than 80% of your tax was taken through PAYE, you also pay towards next year in two payments on account. Each is half of this year’s bill, due 31 January and 31 July.
    4. In your first year this can mean paying the whole year’s bill plus half of next year’s in the same January, so it’s worth putting money aside.
    5. Pay through the HMRC app, online banking using your Self Assessment reference (your 10-digit UTR followed by K), or spread it with a Budget Payment Plan.

    Other help

    • Think the figures are wrong? Contact HMRC before paying.
    • Owe tax but no letter has come? Tell HMRC rather than waiting.
    • Can’t afford to pay in one go? Ask HMRC about a payment plan (Time to Pay).
    • Full details: Tax overpayments and underpayments on GOV.UK.

    HMRC never offers refunds by text, email or WhatsApp links. Get to the HMRC app or GOV.UK yourself, not through a message.

    Good to know

    Usually your main job gets the tax-free allowance and other jobs are taxed from the first pound (often tax code BR at 20%). That’s why a second job can look heavily taxed, and why moving into the higher rate can leave you underpaid.

    National Insurance is worked out separately for each job, so it isn’t shared like tax is. The figures here are yearly estimates; real NI depends on how often you’re paid.

    This app gives estimates only and isn’t connected to or approved by HMRC.

    Not included: pension contributions, student loan, benefits in kind, savings income, Marriage Allowance and the Blind Person’s Allowance. If you think you’ve under or overpaid, check your tax code in the HMRC app or your Personal Tax Account.
